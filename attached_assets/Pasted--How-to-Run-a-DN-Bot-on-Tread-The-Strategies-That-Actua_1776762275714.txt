What is a DN Position?
Imagine you want to buy a spot asset.
Normally, you’re exposed to price movements. If price drops, you lose.
Now what if you could hold that asset but completely remove price risk?
You can do that by:
* 		Buying the asset in spot
* 		
* 		Shorting the same asset in futures
* 		
This creates a delta neutral position — your gains and losses from price cancel each other out.
The Problem
Sounds simple, but execution is where most people fail.
If you:
* 		Market buy spot
* 		
* 		Market sell futures
* 		
You get hit by:
* 		Slippage
* 		
* 		Taker fees
* 		
* 		Poor execution
* 		
Over time, this eats into your profits significantly.
How Tread DN Bot Solves This
The DN bot uses a sophisticated execution algorithm to handle both legs simultaneously.
Instead of large market orders, it:
* 		Splits orders into multiple micro orders
* 		
* 		Executes both sides at the same time
* 		
* 		Minimizes slippage and fees
* 		
Result:
You enter a properly hedged position with efficient pricing and minimal cost.
What Can You Use a DN Bot For?
1) Funding Rate Arbitrage
One of the most popular strategies.
Funding rates can differ across exchanges.
Example:
* 		Exchange A → Positive funding (shorts get paid)
* 		
* 		Exchange B → Negative funding (longs get paid)
* 		
You can:
* 		Short on Exchange A (get paid)
* 		
* 		Long on Exchange B (get paid)
* 		
Your price exposure = ~0
Your profit = funding from both sides
There are tools like loris.tools that help identify these opportunities. I break down exactly how to use it later in this post
2) Cross Pair Trades
You’re:
* 		Bullish on one asset
* 		
* 		Bearish on another
* 		
Instead of taking directional risk, you can:
* 		Long the strong asset
* 		
* 		Short the weak asset
* 		
Both executed simultaneously using the DN bot.
Examples of Pair Trades

* 		
* 		Long ETH / Short BTC

* 		Long BTC / Short weaker altcoins
*

There are countless ways to structure pair trades using the DN bot.
The edge comes from:

Step 5: Set TWAP Duration
This is one of the most important settings.
TWAP duration = how long the bot takes to build your position
It does NOT mean how long the trade stays open.
How to Choose TWAP?
It depends on:
* 		Pair liquidity
* 		
* 		Your position size
* 		
General guideline:
* 		< $10k on liquid pairs → 10–20 minutes works well
* 		
* 		Larger size / illiquid pairs → use longer duration
* 		
Why This Matters
If you choose a short duration:
* 		Bot may use market orders
* 		
* 		Higher fees
* 		
* 		More slippage
* 		
If you choose a longer duration:
* 		Slower execution
* 		
* 		Better pricing
* 		
* 		Lower fees
* 		
So you’re deciding between:
⚡ Faster entry vs 💰 Better execution
Step 6: Position Duration
This defines when the bot will close your position.
* 		Leave empty → runs indefinitely
* 		
* 		Set a time → auto-unwinds after that
* 		
If you don’t set this, you’ll need to close it manually.
Step 7: Set Stop Loss (SL)
This is a safety mechanism.
* 		You define a % distance from liquidation
* 		
* 		If price moves close to liquidation on one leg
* 		
* 		The bot automatically unwinds the entire position
* 		
Why this matters:
* 		Prevents one side from getting liquidated
* 		
* 		Keeps your strategy from breaking
* 		
Step 8: Start the Bot
Click Start
The bot will:

* 		
* 		Execute over your chosen TWAP duration

* 		Optimize for minimal slippage and fees

How Funding Actually Works (Important)
Most people misunderstand this.
* 		Negative funding → Longs get paid
* 		
* 		Positive funding → Shorts get paid
* 		
But here’s the key insight:
The arb doesn’t require one side to be positive and the other negative.
You’re simply looking for:
Where you get paid more on one side than you pay on the other
Extra Edge: Let the Tool Guide Your Execution
When using loris.tools, you don’t have to figure everything out manually.
For each pair, it already shows:
* 		Which exchange to long (buy)
* 		
* 		Which exchange to **short (sell)
* 		
Example 
Check the below case.
* 		On Hyperliquid → -806% funding
* 		→ You get paid to long
* 		

[7]

* 		→ You pay to short
* 		

[8]
So you:
* 		Long on Hyperliquid (earn 806%)
* 		
* 		Short on Aster (pay 500%)
* 		
What’s Your Profit?
Your edge is the difference:
806% (earned) – 500% (paid) = net positive funding
Even though:
* 		Both sides have negative funding
* 		
* 		You’re still profitable
* 		
Key Insight
Funding arb is NOT about:
* 		“Long negative, short positive”
* 		
It’s about:
Capturing the net funding spread
As long as:
* 		What you earn > what you pay
* 		→ You have a profitable setup
* 		
What You Should Look For
* 		High funding differences (300%+ preferred)
* 		
* 		Stable funding (not flipping instantly)
* 		
Why?
Because:
* 		Funding changes fast
* 		
* 		You still pay:Entry fees
* 		Exit fees
* 		
* 		
* 		
Small spreads can get wiped out easily.
