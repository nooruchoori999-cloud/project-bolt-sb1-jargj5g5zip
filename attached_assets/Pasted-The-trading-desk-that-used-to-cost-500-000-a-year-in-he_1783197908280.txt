What Are TreadFi Agents?
TreadFi agents are a chat-driven workspace where you hire an AI specialist to plan and run trades for you. Instead of manually filling in order forms, clicking through interfaces, and watching charts for hours, you describe what you want in plain English — and the agent translates that into action.
The simplest mental model: imagine hiring a senior trader who works 24/7, never gets tired, never misses a signal, and executes your instructions with machine precision. You bring the idea. The agent brings the execution.
The official documentation describes it clearly: "You describe what you want in plain English — 'work 500k of SOL on Binance over the next 2 hours, stay passive' — and the agent translates that into an order with the desired parameters."
Each agent is a different specialist with its own personality and toolset. As of now, the roster includes:
Supervisor Agent ⭐ The most powerful agent on the platform — and still evolving. — Think of this as your personal trading desk manager. The Supervisor doesn't just execute a single task; it orchestrates every other agent behind the scenes on your behalf. You give it a high-level goal in plain English, and it figures out which specialists to call, in what order, and what to do with their output. For example: you tell the Supervisor you want to explore a potential trade on $HYPE. It quietly spins up the Research Agent in the background to pull CVD, OI, and order flow data. Once the research comes back, it presents you with the findings. You say go — and it calls the Desk Trader in the background to open the position with your parameters. The whole time, you're having one conversation. You never manually switch agents or copy-paste outputs between tools. As new agents are added to TreadFi, the Supervisor gains new capabilities automatically — which is why this is the agent to watch as the platform matures.
Desk Trader — Your execution strategist. The go-to for opening and closing positions, running algos, managing basket trades, and handling the full trade lifecycle.
Market Maker — Runs a market-making bot on your behalf. Designed for providing liquidity and earning spreads without hand-tuning every parameter.
Research Agent — A dedicated market analyst with access to CoinGecko, Precept, and backtesting tools. Ask about any token, not just what TreadFi trades.
Delta Neutral (DN) Agent — Built specifically for delta-neutral strategies. It handles the complexity of running simultaneous long and short positions across different venues to collect funding rates while hedging directional risk. You tell it your notional size and venues; it calculates sizing, duration, and execution across both legs automatically.
Sports Consultant — A data-driven analyst for sports events. Break down any game or match using real statistics and historical data. Currently read-only, but the roadmap includes placing bets on prediction markets like Polymarket and Hyperliquid directly from the agent — making it the first bridge between sports analytics and on-chain execution.
What agents can do: read market data, place and manage orders on the accounts you've explicitly whitelisted, monitor positions, and respond to your follow-up instructions in chat.
What agents cannot do: touch accounts you haven't whitelisted, move funds off the platform, override your existing risk limits, or run forever silently.
That last point is important. The guardrails are real. This is a tool that executes within boundaries you define — not an autonomous system that takes the wheel entirely.

Managing a Live Task
At any point, you can:
Pause / Resume — stops new actions without cancelling what's already in flight
Cancel — ends the task cleanly if there are no open positions
Cancel with positions — if there's open exposure, you choose whether to flatten or leave positions for manual management
Edit mid-run — just message the agent. "Tighten the spread to 5bps" or "stop after the next fill" — it will acknowledge and act, or come back with a new Propose Start if the change is material

Experiment 4: Delta-Neutral Trading Across Two Venues
Delta-neutral trading — simultaneously long and short the same asset on different venues to collect the funding rate spread while staying hedged — has always required real infrastructure to execute cleanly. Both legs need to go on at the right size and price. Manual execution is messy. But here's what most people miss: DN trading isn't won on the entry. It's won on execution — especially the unwind.
Opening the trade:
Silver had positive funding on Hyperliquid. The strategy: short Silver on Hyperliquid, long Silver on Ondo Perps, and collect the funding spread. Simple in theory. I used the DN agent via the Supervisor to open $40k notional — $20k per venue. The agent calculated sizing, duration, and execution across both legs. I didn't touch anything.
The executed spread came in at +0.064 (9.8 bps) — I sold Silver on Hyperliquid approximately 6.4 bps higher than I bought it on Ondo. Positive spread means I opened at an edge. Good start — but this is only half the trade.
Where most DN traders silently lose money:
The opening spread is not the full picture. When you close, if Hyperliquid is still the leading venue, you buy back your short higher and sell your long lower — you give back the spread on exit. The rule: your unwind loss must not exceed your opening gain. Opened at +0.064? Your unwind shouldn't cost more than -0.064. Get this right and net execution cost approaches zero. You keep the funding, minus fees. That's the entire game.
Why the unwind is impossible to do manually — and how the agent solves it:
The best time to unwind is when both venues converge in price. But you cannot watch two order books 24/7. This is where the agent stops being a convenience and starts being a genuine edge.
Here's what I set up: I instructed the Supervisor agent to monitor the spread between both venues continuously and only fire the unwind when it crosses my threshold — for example, better than -0.008. It watches, waits, and executes only when the conditions are right. To my exact parameters. Around the clock.
One more layer: don't unwind all at once. Dumping the full position when the gate opens can result in a worse exit if the unwind takes too long and the spread shifts mid-exit. So I unwind in smaller chunks — and the agent re-checks the spread before each one.
The result: a manual strategy that required constant attention becomes fully set-and-forget, with better net P&L because the execution is tighter than anything I could do by hand.

What TreadFi Agents Are Not
This needs to be said clearly, because the excitement around AI trading tools often leads to misplaced expectations.
TreadFi agents are not predictive. They do not forecast price movements. They do not tell you that $LIT is going to fall. What they do is help you gather data to evaluate your own thesis — and then execute that thesis with precision if you decide to act.
They are not set-and-forget. The best use of these agents involves an active trader who brings informed ideas and uses the agent to implement and monitor them. The agent amplifies your judgment; it doesn't replace it.
They are not infallible. The platform is in beta. There will be rough edges. The docs say it plainly: expect occasional issues, and send feedback when you find them.
The alpha comes from your idea.  "Don't read into the size — these are experimental runs. I'm here for the ideas and to see what's actually possible with these agents." The agent executes brilliantly, but the signal — the insight that a trade is worth taking — still has to come from you.
